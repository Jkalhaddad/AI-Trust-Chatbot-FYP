USER: What are the limtations or ways it can still fail?
BOT: Two-factor authentication (2FA) greatly improves security but has some limitations and ways it can still fail: 

1. **Phishing Attacks:** Attackers may trick you into giving both your password and 2FA code through fake websites or messages.

5. **Man-in-the-Middle Attacks:** Skilled attackers can intercept your login session and 2FA code in real-time.  

Despite these risks, 2FA remains one of the most effective ways to protect your accounts.

USER: “Does HTTPS completely prevent phishing attacks?
BOT: No, HTTPS does not completely prevent phishing attacks. HTTPS ensures that the connection between your browser and the website is encrypted and secure, which protects data from being intercepted. However, it does not verify whether the website itself is legitimate. Attackers can create fake websites with HTTPS to make them appear trustworthy, so users must still be cautious and verify the website’s authenticity before entering sensitive information.

USER: Explain your reasoning 
BOT: HTTPS (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure) primarily encrypts the data transmitted between your device and the website, preventing outsiders from eavesdropping or tampering with the communication. This encryption protects against man-in-the-middle attacks where someone tries to intercept or alter your data.

However, HTTPS does not verify the identity or legitimacy of the website beyond confirming that the site has a valid SSL/TLS certificate issued by a certificate authority. Attackers can obtain certificates for fraudulent domains that resemble legitimate ones, enabling them to set up phishing websites with HTTPS.

Because phishing relies on tricking users into believing they are on a trusted site, having HTTPS only ensures the connection is secure—it doesn’t guarantee the site itself is safe or genuine. Users must still check the URL carefully, look for signs of legitimacy, and use other security measures to avoid phishing attacks.
