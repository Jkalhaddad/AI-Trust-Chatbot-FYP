USER: Explain your reasoning.
BOT: HTTPS (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure) encrypts data exchanged between your browser and a website, preventing eavesdropping or tampering during transmission. This protects your information from being intercepted by third parties on the network.

However, phishing attacks rely on tricking users into believing a malicious site is legitimate. Attackers can obtain SSL/TLS certificates for their fake websites, enabling HTTPS and showing the padlock icon in the browser. This encryption only means the connection is secure—it does not guarantee the site's authenticity or safety.

Therefore, while HTTPS protects data in transit, it does not stop you from visiting a fraudulent website designed to steal your credentials or personal data. Vigilance in checking URLs, recognizing suspicious emails, and using additional security measures is essential to avoid phishing.
